How to choose a robot for industrial internal transport

Start with the route your team repeats: origin and destination, trips per shift, load per trip and waiting time. An internal logistics robot must fit that workflow, whether in a warehouse, a hotel laundry or a hospital supply area.

Payload, passage width and delivery points

Compare the KEENON S100 and KEENON S300 against the actual weight, volume and stability of the material and the loading and unloading process. Check turns, crossings, slopes and passage widths with the proposed configuration. The S100's published payload is 100 kg+; the permitted load is confirmed for each configuration.

Integration and a route trial

If the route includes doors or lifts, we check compatibility before confirming an automated route. The pilot records completed trips, manual interventions, waiting times and availability. Hours freed up are valued using actual operating costs to estimate the return, without assuming that every automated hour becomes a cash saving.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about internal logistics.

What is the difference between the S100 and S300?

The S100 is geared towards medium loads and agile routes; the S300 is built for heavy loads and intensive logistics operations.

Can it work in hospitals or laundries?

Yes, as long as the assessment validates routes, loading points, aisle width, elevators, door interaction and internal protocols.
